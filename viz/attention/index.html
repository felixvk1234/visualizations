Transformers · The core mechanism

Attention, explained slowly

Attention has a scary reputation, but underneath it is one simple idea: each word builds its meaning by taking a weighted average of the other words. Everything else — queries, keys, values, softmax — is just the machinery for choosing good weights. This page walks through that machinery one small step at a time. No step requires more than multiplying and adding a few numbers.

Before any math — the problem

A word alone doesn't know what it means

Read the word “bank” in these two sentences:

“She sat on the river bank.”

Here “bank” means the side of a river — because of “river”.

“She opened a savings account at the bank.”

Here “bank” means a financial institution — because of “savings account”.

The letters b-a-n-k are identical; only the surrounding words decide the meaning. So a good language model needs a mechanism that lets each word look at the other words and pull in the relevant ones. That mechanism is attention. Keep this picture in mind: “bank” reaching over to “river” and borrowing some of its meaning.

The cast of characters

Every word plays three roles at once

To let words look things up in each other, attention gives every word three separate vectors, made by multiplying its embedding with three learned matrices. The names are abstract, but each role has a very concrete job. A library analogy:

Q Query — “what am I looking for?”

The question a word asks about its context. The word “bank” might ask: “is there a word nearby that tells me which kind of bank I am?”

K Key — “what can I be found by?”

The label on the spine of the book. The word “river” advertises: “I'm nature/water-related — match me if that's what you need.”

V Value — “what do I hand over if chosen?”

The content inside the book. Once “river” is matched, its value is the actual information that flows into “bank”.

Why three roles, not one? What a word searches for, what it can be found by, and what it contributes are different things. “River” is found by water-related questions (key), but what it contributes (value) is its meaning. Keeping the roles separate gives the model freedom to learn each job independently.

The walkthrough — one step at a time

Six tiny steps, one tiny sentence

Build intuition — play with it

The dot product is a “compatibility meter”

The score between a query and a key is their dot product: multiply matching coordinates and add them up. Geometrically, vectors pointing the same way score high, perpendicular vectors score near zero, and opposite vectors score negative. Zooming back out

All words attend at once — that's the famous formula

We followed one word (“slept”) for clarity, but in reality every word runs the same six steps simultaneously. Stack all the queries into a matrix \(Q\), the keys into \(K\), the values into \(V\), and the whole walkthrough collapses into one line:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \underbrace{\text{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big)}_{\text{the attention weights}}\,V$$

The softmax term is a table of weights: one row per asking word, one column per answering word, each row summing to 100%. Here is that table for our toy sentence — row “slept” contains exactly the weights you saw in the walkthrough:

How to read it

Pick a row, e.g. slept. Walk across it: “slept” gives 13% of its attention to “the”, 31% to “fluffy”, 45% to “cat”, 11% to itself. Every row is one word's complete attention budget.

Notice fluffy's row: it attends mostly to cat — adjectives look at the nouns they describe. Nobody programmed that; in a trained model these patterns emerge from data.

One last layer of the onion

Multi-head: several attentions in parallel

One attention pattern can only encode one kind of relationship at a time. So transformers run several attention “heads” side by side — each with its own \(W_Q, W_K, W_V\) matrices, each free to learn a different pattern — and concatenate the results. A real ViT-Base or GPT-style layer has 12 such heads, and 12+ layers of them stacked. But every single head, in every single layer, is exactly the six steps you walked through above — nothing more.

If you remember three things

The whole page in three sentences

Attention = weighted averaging

Each word's new representation is a mix of all words' values. The cleverness is entirely in choosing the mixing weights.

Weights come from Q·K matches

A word's query is compared to every word's key with a dot product; softmax turns the scores into percentages that sum to 100%.

Everything else is repetition

Multiple heads, multiple layers, images instead of words — all of it is this same six-step recipe, run many times in parallel and in sequence.
