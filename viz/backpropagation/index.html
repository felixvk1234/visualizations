Training · How networks learn

Backpropagation, explained simply

A neural network learns the way you'd tune a radio with a thousand dials: make a guess, measure how wrong it was, and figure out which way to turn each dial to be a little less wrong. Backpropagation is the trick that finds the right direction for every dial at once. Three pictures are enough to understand it.

Picture 1 · One weight

Learning is walking downhill

Forget the network for a second — imagine it has just one weight. For every value of that weight, the network makes some error (the loss). Plot loss against weight and you get a valley. Learning means walking to the bottom. The gradient is simply the slope under your feet — it tells you which way is downhill and how steep it is. Drag the ball, then take steps:

weight w2.60
loss L(w)1.22
slope (gradient)+1.50
so move…← left

Step size (learning rate η)

Each step moves the weight against the slope: downhill.

$$w \;\leftarrow\; w - \eta \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}}_{\text{the slope}}$$

Try the reckless step size. With η = 2.1 each step overshoots the bottom and lands higher on the other side — the loss explodes. This one-line update rule, with a sensible η, is the entire learning algorithm. The only open question is: with millions of weights, how do we get the slope for each one? That's backpropagation.

Picture 2 · The backward pass

The error flows backward and assigns blame

Here is a real network with six weights, computed live. Watch one full learning step in four moves — click Next:

Why “back”-propagation? A weight deep in the network affects the loss only through everything after it. The chain rule says: multiply the slopes along the path. Starting from the error and sweeping right-to-left, each layer reuses the slopes already computed to its right — so one backward sweep prices every weight, no matter how many millions there are.

If you remember three things

The whole page in three sentences

The gradient is a slope

For each weight: “if I nudge this up, does the error grow or shrink?” Learning = step against the slope, scaled by the learning rate.

Backprop assigns blame

One backward sweep through the network gives every weight its share of the error, using the chain rule: multiply the slopes along the path.

Training is just repetition

Predict, compare, backpropagate, nudge — a tiny improvement each time, repeated millions of times. That's all “training” means.
