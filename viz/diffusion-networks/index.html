Diffusion Networks

The models behind Stable Diffusion, DALL·E and Midjourney are built on a strange idea: to learn how to create images, first learn how to destroy them. A diffusion network watches photos dissolve into pure noise, then trains a neural network to run that film backwards — one small denoising step at a time. Follow one photo of a ginger cat through the whole story. Every noisy image on this page is computed live from the photo's real pixels.

The full picture

Destroy with noise, learn to rebuild

Two processes, mirror images of each other. The forward process is fixed and brainless: it just sprinkles Gaussian noise on the image, over and over, until nothing is left. The reverse process is where all the intelligence lives: a neural network learns to undo one noising step at a time. Each stage below is explained in detail further down the page.

Step 1 — The forward process

Drowning a cat in Gaussian noise

The forward process blends the image with random noise, a little more at every timestep \(t\). Conveniently, you never have to apply the steps one by one — there is a closed form that jumps straight to any timestep. The number \(\bar\alpha_t\) (between 1 and 0) sets the mix: how much original image survives versus how much noise has taken over. Drag the slider to move through time yourself.

$$x_t \;=\; \underbrace{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0}_{\text{what's left of the cat}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\;\epsilon}_{\text{noise}}, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$

Step 2 — Training

The network's only job: spot the noise

Here is the trick that makes diffusion work. Generating an image from scratch is hopelessly hard — but guessing which pixels are noise in a corrupted image is a concrete, learnable task. Training is just this, millions of times: take a photo, noise it to a random timestep, show the network \(x_t\) and \(t\), and grade its guess \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\) against the true noise \(\epsilon\):

$$\mathcal{L} \;=\; \big\lVert\, \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,\, t) \,\big\rVert^2$$

If the network can name the noise, it implicitly knows what the clean image was — solve the forward equation for \(x_0\) and you can look through the noise. Drag the training slider to see how the network's view of the cat sharpens as its noise predictions improve.

An untrained network guesses random noise, so subtracting its guess reveals garbage. As training improves the prediction \(\hat\epsilon\) (simulated here by blending toward the true \(\epsilon\)), the implied clean image converges to the cat — even though the network never outputs an image directly.

Step 3 — Sampling

Generation is denoising in a loop

To create an image, the trained model starts from pure random noise — no photo anywhere — and applies its denoising step over and over: predict the noise, remove a sliver of it, repeat. Structure emerges gradually: first vague colour fields, then shapes, then fur and whiskers. Press generate and watch all \(T\) steps run.

$$x_{t-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\;\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\right) + \sigma_t z$$

Why it matters

Three things to remember

Hard problem, easy pieces

“Paint a cat from nothing” is intractable; “remove a little noise” is almost trivial. Diffusion splits one impossible leap into a thousand easy steps — and a network that masters the easy step inherits the impossible one.

Noise is the canvas

Every generated image is born as pure Gaussian noise. The starting noise acts like a random seed: different draws of \(x_T\) flow back to different images, so one model yields endless variety.

Add text, get DALL·E

Condition the noise predictor on a caption embedding — \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \text{“a ginger cat”})\) — and the denoising walk is steered toward images matching the text. That single change turns this page into a text-to-image system.
