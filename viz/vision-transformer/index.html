The Vision Transformer

A transformer doesn't know what an image is — it only understands sequences of tokens, like words in a sentence. The Vision Transformer (ViT) makes images speak that language: it cuts a photo into patches, treats each patch as a word, and lets self-attention figure out how the pieces relate. Step 1 — Patchify

An image becomes a sequence of “visual words”

Transformers were built for text, where the input is naturally a sequence. An image is just a grid of pixels — so ViT imposes a sequence on it by slicing the image into fixed-size square patches, read left-to-right, top-to-bottom, like words on a page. Drag the slider to change the patch size, and toggle Explode to see the patches as separate tokens. Your choice here carries through every later step of this page — patch size is the one design decision that fixes the sequence length the rest of the model has to work with.

The original ViT uses 16 × 16-pixel patches on a 224 × 224 image — a 14 × 14 grid, i.e. 196 tokens. That's where the paper's title comes from: “An Image is Worth 16×16 Words.”

Step 2 — Embedding

Each patch is flattened into a vector

Carrying over your Step 1 choice: 4 × 4 grid → 16 patch tokens (+1 CLS)

A transformer can't eat image tiles directly — every token must be a vector of numbers. Each patch (16 × 16 pixels × 3 colour channels = 768 raw values) is flattened and multiplied by a learned matrix \(\mathbf{E}\), projecting it to the model dimension \(D\). Two extras are then added:

  • CLS token — a learned vector prepended to the sequence. It carries no image content itself; its job is to collect a summary of the whole image as it passes through the encoder.
  • Position embeddings — patches alone don't say where they came from. A learned position vector is added to each token so the model knows patch #1 is the top-left corner and patch #196 is the bottom-right.
$$\mathbf{z}_0 = \big[\,\mathbf{x}_{\text{cls}};\; \mathbf{x}^1_p\mathbf{E};\; \mathbf{x}^2_p\mathbf{E};\; \cdots;\; \mathbf{x}^N_p\mathbf{E}\,\big] + \mathbf{E}_{pos}$$

The input sequence for our puppy photo, using your 4 × 4 grid from Step 1. Step 3 — Self-attention

Every patch looks at every other patch

Still your Step 1 choice: 4 × 4 grid — every patch can attend to all 16

This is the heart of the transformer. For each token, attention computes a query, compares it against the keys of all other tokens, and turns the match scores into weights with a softmax:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

High weight = “this patch is relevant to me.” A patch on the puppy's ear can directly consult a patch on its paw in a single step — no matter how far apart they are in the image. A convolutional network would need many stacked layers to connect pixels that distant; attention does it immediately. For illustration, this head's weights are computed live from the photo: patches with similar colour and texture statistics attend to each other more, with a mild distance penalty — a pattern real ViT heads often learn. A trained model has 12 heads per layer, each learning its own pattern.

Step 4 — The encoder block

Attention + MLP, repeated 12 times

One encoder block does two things: self-attention lets tokens exchange information, then a small MLP lets each token process what it just gathered. Both are wrapped with LayerNorm and a residual (skip) connection, which keep training stable in deep stacks. ViT-Base repeats this block 12 times — by the final layer, every token has mixed information from the entire image many times over.

Step 5 — Classification

The CLS token delivers the verdict

After 12 rounds of attention, the CLS token — which started as a blank, learned vector — has absorbed a summary of the entire image. A single small MLP head maps it to one score per class, and a softmax turns the scores into probabilities. Everything before this point never saw a class label directly; the head is the only part that speaks “dog.”

Why it matters

Three things to remember

Images as language

ViT's only image-specific step is the patchify stage. After that, it's a standard text transformer — the same architecture behind GPT and BERT — which is why advances transfer freely between vision and NLP.

Global from layer one

A CNN builds up its field of view slowly, layer by layer. In a ViT, the very first attention layer already connects opposite corners of the image — ear, paw, and grass can interact immediately.

Scale is the trade-off

ViT bakes in fewer assumptions about images than a CNN, so it needs more data to learn them — but given large-scale pre-training, it matches or beats the best CNNs, and dominates as models grow.
